How it works

1

Say what you're here for

YouTube's homepage asks what brings you here. Ask the way you'd ask a librarian: “Find a good tutorial for building a timber frame porch.” That's your aim.

2

Aim keeps YouTube on it

Shorts, recommendations, autoplay and the feeds step aside while you're on your aim. If a video seems to wander off, Aim asks first. You can always say yes.

3

Take a break whenever you like

Breaks are free browsing from a daily allowance you choose: 15 minutes, an hour, or just one video. When a break ends, you're right back where you were.

Learn · Explain · Apply

Watching isn't understanding.

After a stretch of videos, a practice student asks you one question about what you watched, and you explain it in your own words. Out loud if you like. “I don't know” is a fine answer; it shows where to look next.

  • What you couldn't explain yet becomes your next video, and then a follow-up question.
  • When it's something to do, Aim suggests a small way to try it: a joint in scrap pine, a circuit on a breadboard.
  • About a week later, one quick question checks it stuck.
  • No scores, no streaks. What you watched, explained and applied is kept apart, as evidence.

Your library

Every goal becomes a book.

A quick search stays a search. When you're working toward something, Aim keeps it as a book: what it takes, what you've explained, what you tried, your notes and the videos that helped. Pick any of them up where you left off.

Start from what you already follow: Aim can sort your subscriptions into the subjects you care about, with your favorite channels first when it suggests videos. Your subscriptions on YouTube don't change.

Privacy

Your learning stays yours.

Without an account, Aim has no server, no analytics and no AI: what you search, watch and write stays in your browser. With an account, a feature sends only what it needs, when you use it. With Aim Plus, sync keeps your library on Aim's server, if you turn it on, so your other computers have it too.

Questions

Does Aim block YouTube?

No. It keeps YouTube on what you came for, and asks before you wander off. Breaks are always one tap away, and you decide how long they are.

Which browsers does it work in?

Chrome on a computer, and browsers that install from the Chrome Web Store, such as Microsoft Edge and Brave. Not on phones or the YouTube app.

Do I need an account?

No. Aim works fully without one. A free account adds the librarian and a daily TeachBack conversation; Aim Plus adds more.

Will it mark my progress with scores?

No. Aim keeps evidence instead: what you watched, what you explained in your own words, and what you tried. It never claims to measure how much you understand.

Is there a version for homeschooling?

Aim Homeschool is coming later: weekly plans from your own curriculum that you approve, and a focused school day on YouTube for each student. We're building it with a few families first.

Is Aim made by YouTube?

No. Aim is independent and isn't affiliated with YouTube or Google.
